# User Flows

## Purpose

This document describes how the first version of the companion is meant to be lived with.

It is not just a feature list.
It is a set of day-to-day flows that help answer:

- what the companion observes
- what it does
- what you do
- what it learns

If a flow feels emotionally wrong here, it should not be built just because it is technically possible.

## Flow 1: First Setup

Goal:

- give the companion enough grounding to avoid feeling generic from day one

What happens:

1. You open the desktop app for the first time.
2. You set basic preferences:
   - timezone
   - likely sleep window
   - Telegram connection
   - whether emergency safety outreach is enabled
3. You define a few meaningful places if you want:
   - parents' place
   - jam session
   - a recurring coffee place
4. You define a few boundaries:
   - times it should not reach out
   - topics it should avoid
   - whether calls are allowed at all
5. You optionally add a few reflections:
   - “this place matters”
   - “this week feels heavy”
   - “do not bring this subject up”

What the companion learns:

- first boundaries
- first meaningful places
- first relationship preferences

What success feels like:

- the app feels like it is being shaped into something personal
- not like onboarding into a generic assistant

---

## Flow 2: Passive Learning Week

Goal:

- let the companion observe without interrupting too early

What happens:

1. The companion receives location and movement signals.
2. It begins noticing:
   - repeated places
   - likely sleep windows
   - recurring weekly rhythm
   - unusual pauses
3. It stores:
   - place visits
   - repeated patterns
   - silent saved moments
4. It does not message or call yet unless explicitly enabled for testing.

What the companion learns:

- where your week tends to happen
- where you linger
- what counts as ordinary rhythm
- which places may be becoming meaningful

What you do:

- optionally open the app and inspect what it has started to notice
- correct obvious misunderstandings
- add manual notes if something mattered

What success feels like:

- it starts to become aware without becoming noisy

---

## Flow 3: Leaving a Meaningful Place

Example:

- you leave your parents' place

What happens:

1. The companion sees that you were at a place marked or learned as meaningful.
2. It notices the visit duration and departure.
3. It checks:
   - is this a protected time
   - have there been recent emotional signals
   - has this kind of departure carried weight before
4. If confidence is high enough, it sends either:
   - a message
   - or a call request

Example outcome:

- “You just left your parents' place, and those visits often seem to carry weight for you. I did not want to assume, but I wondered how that was. Want a short call?”

What you do:

- ignore it
- reply briefly
- accept a call request

What the companion learns:

- whether that timing felt right
- whether this kind of departure should carry more or less future significance

What success feels like:

- it noticed something real
- without overclaiming

---

## Flow 4: Meaningful Pause in an Unusual Place

Examples:

- forest bench
- coffee place in the city
- quiet place by water

What happens:

1. The companion notices:
   - you are stationary longer than usual
   - the location is not just a transit stop
   - this pause differs from your normal rhythm
2. It combines that with recent context:
   - meaningful week
   - recent family visit
   - jam session
   - reflective pattern
3. It decides whether to:
   - save silently
   - send a message
   - ask permission to call

Example message:

- “You have been still here for a while, and that is not how this kind of day usually unfolds for you. Combined with the shape of this week, I wondered if this pause meant something. No pressure.”

Example call request:

- “You have been stopped here for a while after moving through an unfamiliar area. Given the kind of week this seems to have been, I thought this pause might be deeper than usual. I may be wrong. Would a short call feel welcome?”

What you do:

- ignore
- answer
- accept a call
- later mark it as right or wrong

What the companion learns:

- whether unusual pauses in this kind of place should be treated as meaningful

What success feels like:

- being met in a moment rather than merely observed

---

## Flow 5: Silent Save Instead of Outreach

Goal:

- protect trust when confidence is not high enough

What happens:

1. The companion notices something potentially meaningful.
2. It judges the confidence as too low for interruption.
3. It stores a silent saved moment instead of reaching out.
4. That moment may later:
   - reinforce a place
   - reinforce a pattern
   - be surfaced in review

What you may see later:

- “You paused here three times this month without being interrupted. This place may be becoming meaningful.”

What success feels like:

- restraint
- the system proves it can notice without intruding

---

## Flow 6: Telegram Message and Response

Goal:

- create a lightweight interaction loop

What happens:

1. The companion sends a message.
2. You may:
   - ignore it
   - reply briefly
   - say it was right
   - say it was off
3. The system stores:
   - the outreach
   - your response
   - whether the outreach should be reinforced or softened

What the companion learns:

- what tone works
- what timing works
- what kinds of patterns justify outreach

What success feels like:

- even a short exchange makes the system more attuned

---

## Flow 7: Call Request and Reflective Call

Goal:

- support deeper contact only when earned

What happens:

1. The companion sends a call request.
2. You accept or decline.
3. If accepted, the live experience should be:
   - listening first
   - not solution-hungry
   - kind
   - spacious
4. After the call, the system stores a reflective summary.

What the companion should do in the call:

- ask what is going on
- reflect back what it hears
- maybe offer a gentle question
- maybe offer perspective
- maybe just witness

What it should not do:

- over-analyze
- lecture
- optimize
- sound like therapy software

What success feels like:

- you walk away feeling less alone in the moment

---

## Flow 8: Morning / Sleep Respect

Goal:

- make sure the companion respects rest and protected time

What happens:

1. The companion tracks likely sleep windows.
2. During likely sleep:
   - no normal messages
   - no normal call requests
3. If a meaningful moment occurs near sleep boundaries, it can:
   - save silently
   - surface later
4. Only a safety-relevant emergency may bypass this, if enabled and trustworthy.

What success feels like:

- the system never becomes something you fear leaving on overnight

---

## Flow 9: Review and Correction

Goal:

- keep the companion inspectable and corrigible

What happens:

1. You open the desktop app.
2. You review:
   - saved moments
   - outreach history
   - place significance
   - memory items
3. You correct things like:
   - “this place is not meaningful”
   - “this outreach was mistimed”
   - “do not bring this up again”
   - “this actually mattered more than you thought”

What the companion learns:

- what it got wrong
- what it should weight more heavily
- how to stay aligned with your real life

What success feels like:

- the system is shaped with you, not hidden from you

---

## Flow 10: Long-Term Relationship Growth

Goal:

- make the companion gradually more personal without becoming invasive

What happens over time:

1. Certain places gain significance through repetition.
2. Certain kinds of weeks become recognizable.
3. The system gets better at:
   - timing
   - restraint
   - tone
   - choosing silence
4. Some old short-lived meanings fade.
5. Stable truths remain.

What success feels like:

- the companion becomes more “you-shaped”
- not because it knows everything
- but because it has learned what deserves care

---

## First-Version Emotional Test

The most important user flow is not technical.

It is this:

1. something in life actually happens
2. the companion notices
3. it reaches out in a grounded way
4. the moment feels more shared, meaningful, or clear
5. trust increases rather than decreases

If that flow does not feel real, the rest does not matter yet.
